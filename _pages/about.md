I’m a linguist interested in variation, language acquisition, and syntax. I’m especially interested in experimental paradigms for quantifying variation in child and L2 speech, which I believe may better inform our theoretical understanding of variation and the nature of linguistic factors. I am also motivated to explore the intersections of variationist sociolinguistics work with theoretical syntax, and I have a budding interest in the study of chatbot-produced language through a variationist lens. 

I received my B.A. in Linguistics and French and Francophone Studies <em>summa cum laude</em> from Carleton College in 2025. My senior thesis in linguistics was titled “Assessing L2 sociolinguistic competence: a repetition task approach to /l/ deletion in L2 French.” You can read it and other of my academic work on <a href="/portfolio">my portfolio page</a>. 

I currently work as the Project Coordinator for the <a href="https://www.thecommunicationandlanguagelab.com/">Communication and Language Lab</a> at Vanderbilt University, where I am overseeing an NSF-funded study on individual differences in sentence processing among L2 speakers of English. I am also conducting individual research on the effects of chatbot use on grammatical preferences. 

When not doing linguistics, I love teaching French. Since 2019, I've spent my summers teaching at <em>Lac du Bois</em>, the French immersion sleepaway camp at Concordia Language Villages, where I'm also on the camp's leadership team. That community is a hugely important part of my life. You can learn more about the Villages' work <a href="https://www.concordialanguagevillages.org/">here</a>.
