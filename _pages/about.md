I’m a linguist interested in variation, language acquisition, and syntax. I’m especially interested in experimental paradigms for quantifying variation in child and L2 speech, which I believe may better inform our theoretical understanding of variation and the nature of linguistic factors. I am also motivated to explore the intersections of variationist sociolinguistics work with theoretical syntax, and I have a budding interest in the study of chatbot-produced language through a variationist lens. 

I received my B.A. in Linguistics and French and Francophone Studies <em>summa cum laude</em> from Carleton College in 2025. My senior thesis in linguistics was titled “Assessing L2 sociolinguistic competence: a repetition task approach to /l/ deletion in L2 French.” You can read it and other of my academic work on my profile. 

I currently work as the Project Coordinator for the Communication and Language Lab at Vanderbilt University, where I am overseeing an NSF-funded study on individual differences in sentence processing among L2 speakers of English. I am also conducting individual research on the effects of chatbot use on grammatical preferences. 

When not doing linguistics, I love to teach. I worked as a teaching assistant and tutor in both linguistics and French throughout college, and I've spent the past seven summers teaching French at Concordia Language Villages, where I am also a member of the camp's leadership staff.
